# China-Origin Chains Top 1,100 US Storefronts, Independent Census Finds

**chainsfromchina.com publishes a continuously verified census of China-origin retail and restaurant brands on the ground in the US, now totaling 1,147 outlets across 47 states, Washington, DC, and Puerto Rico.**

FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE · November 28, 2026 · [chainsfromchina.com](https://chainsfromchina.com)

November 28, 2026 — Chains From China, an independent tracker of China-origin retail and restaurant brands (companies founded, headquartered, and scaled in China) operating in the United States, today reported that it now counts **89 chains running 1,147 US outlets**, the most complete, continuously verified census of the sector published to date.

The brands reach **47 states, Washington, DC, and Puerto Rico**, absent only from Alaska, Vermont, and Wyoming.

## The findings at a glance

- **Scale and spread.** 89 brands now run 1,147 outlets. The density is still greatest in the big coastal metros, but coverage extends across the Midwest, the South, and the Mountain West.
- **Two brands dominate.** Miniso and Pop Mart alone account for about 45% of all US outlets (515 of 1,147), and few shoppers would peg either as China-origin. At the other end, 30 of the 89 chains have just a single US store, and about half have two or fewer.
- **The shakeout is already underway.** New entrants arrive almost weekly: recent first US openings include Bingxue, Walaida, and Zhengxin Chicken Steak. Yet early movers are already retrenching, and some have left altogether. At least six chains that opened in the US have since closed every store they had here, and eight more are down to a single location after closing others. Möge Tee, a bubble-tea chain, has closed at least 38 of its US stores. MLBB (Malubianbian), a Sichuan hotpot chain, has closed at least seven US restaurants and has one left.
- **They arrive in clusters.** China-origin brands increasingly co-locate. Twenty-two of them cluster in and around the Tangram complex in Flushing, Queens, and in Allston, Boston, Molly Tea, Cotti Coffee, and Zhengxin Chicken Steak share one food hall.
- **Far beyond bubble tea.** The roster runs from tea and hotpot to rice noodles, bakeries, variety retail, apparel (JNBY, EP Yaying), and jewelry (Lao Feng Xiang).

## Where the chains concentrate

- **Nearly half the map is two states.** California (333) and New York (181) hold 514 of the 1,147 US outlets between them, about 45%. Three metros — Greater New York, Los Angeles, and the San Francisco Bay Area — account for about 44% on their own.
- **Retail giants, but a fragmented food scene.** Variety and lifestyle stores from just seven brands, led by Miniso and Pop Mart, account for 538 outlets (47%). Food and drink accounts for 594 (52%), spread across 72 brands, 247 of them tea shops. Apparel, pharmacy, and jewelry make up the remaining 15.
- **Clustered where the customers already are.** A modeled analysis by the tracker (using US Census and university-location data, in aggregate only) places the median outlet in a neighborhood about 16.1% Asian, roughly 2.6 times the national average, and just over a mile from the nearest college campus.

“Most Americans have probably walked past one of these stores without knowing where the brand came from. We count 89 China-origin chains running more than 1,100 US storefronts, and that includes only what we could verify,” said Daniel Silver, founder of Twoshoe Labs, which publishes Chains From China. “This is no longer a trend story. It is an industry, and we could not find a store-by-store count of it, so we built one.”

“There are really two stories here, and they are not only about size. Some of these brands are built for everyone: the giants Miniso and Pop Mart, the tea and coffee chains, and even apparel brands that are big in China but still small in the US, like Balabala, JNBY, and EP Yaying, all opening in ordinary malls where most shoppers have no idea they are Chinese. Others, especially some restaurants, still cluster where Chinese and Asian communities already live, and the real question is which of them cross over,” Silver added.

## How the count is built

Chains From China compiles its census from two streams. Each day an automated collector reads the chains' own published store locators; separately, individual locations are confirmed by hand, through on-the-ground visits, operator-verified listings, and primary-source filings such as franchise disclosure documents. The two streams are complementary: automated collection provides breadth, while human verification provides the accuracy, resolving the namesakes, phantom listings, and scope questions that an automated pass cannot settle on its own. Notable observations and status changes, such as new openings, closures, and count revisions, are recorded in a tamper-evident, hash-chained provenance ledger, and most coordinates come from the US Census geocoder.

The count is deliberately conservative. Announced-but-unopened stores, automated vending kiosks, and locations verified as closed are all tracked, but kept out of the headline figure. The 1,147 count is a floor, not a ceiling. It includes only verified locations, and it keeps climbing as new stores open. The tracker is in beta, and its counts and map are updated continuously.

The full methodology is published at [chainsfromchina.com/method.html](https://chainsfromchina.com/method.html).

## A free public map, and more to come

The full census is browsable as a free interactive map at [chainsfromchina.com](https://chainsfromchina.com), which plots every outlet, lets readers filter by brand and sector, and surfaces the clusters where China-origin chains gather. A Pro tier adds revenue and capacity estimates, store-count comparisons against the brands' home markets, and co-tenancy analysis for operators, investors, and landlords.

The tracker updates daily, and Chains From China will issue periodic “state of the sector” briefings as the count moves. Reporters are welcome to cite the live figures; the public map and the headline counts are, and will remain, free.

## About Chains From China

Chains From China is an independent commercial research project that tracks China-origin retail and restaurant brands (companies founded, headquartered, and scaled in China) as they expand in the United States. It is a project of Twoshoe Labs LLC and is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or sponsored by any company it measures; brand names are used only to identify the businesses being tracked.
