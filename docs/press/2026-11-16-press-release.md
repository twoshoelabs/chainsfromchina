# China-Origin Chains Pass 1,000 US Storefronts, Independent Census Finds

**chainsfromchina.com publishes a continuously verified census of China-origin retail and restaurant brands on the ground in the US, now totaling 1,152 outlets across 47 states, Washington, DC, and Puerto Rico.**

FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE · November 16, 2026 · [chainsfromchina.com](https://chainsfromchina.com)

November 16, 2026 — Chains From China, an independent tracker of China-origin retail and restaurant brands (companies founded, headquartered, and scaled in China) operating in the United States, today reported that it now counts **86 chains running 1,152 US outlets**, the most complete, continuously verified census of the sector published to date.

The brands reach **47 states, Washington, DC, and Puerto Rico**, absent only from Alaska, Vermont, and Wyoming.

## The findings at a glance

- **Scale and spread.** 86 brands now run 1,152 outlets. The density is still greatest in the big coastal metros, but coverage extends across the Midwest, the South, and the Mountain West.
- **Two brands dominate.** Miniso and Pop Mart alone account for 45% of all US outlets (513 of 1,152), and few shoppers would peg either as China-origin. At the other end, 28 of the 86 chains have opened just a single US store, and more than half have two or fewer.
- **The shakeout is already underway.** New entrants arrive almost weekly: recent first US openings include Bingxue, Walaida, and Zhengxin Chicken Steak. Yet early movers are already retrenching. One bubble-tea chain is down to 44 US stores after closing at least 35, nearly half of all it opened. One Sichuan hotpot chain has shrunk from nine US locations to just one.
- **They arrive in clusters.** China-origin brands increasingly co-locate. Eighteen of them share the Tangram complex in Flushing, Queens, and in Allston, Boston, Molly Tea, Cotti Coffee, and Zhengxin Chicken Steak share one food hall.
- **Far beyond bubble tea.** The roster runs from tea and hotpot to rice noodles, bakeries, variety retail, apparel (JNBY, EP Yaying), and jewelry (Lao Feng Xiang).

## Where the chains concentrate

- **Half the map is two states.** California (346) and New York (177) hold 523 of the 1,152 US outlets between them, just under half. Three metros — Greater New York, Los Angeles, and the San Francisco Bay Area — account for 44% on their own.
- **Retail giants, but a fragmented food scene.** About half the outlets (535) are variety and lifestyle stores from just seven brands, led by Miniso and Pop Mart. The other half is food and drink, spread across roughly 60 brands, 261 of them tea shops.
- **Clustered where the customers already are.** A modeled analysis by the tracker (using US Census and university-location data, in aggregate only) places the median outlet in a neighborhood about 16.4% Asian, roughly 2.6 times the national average, and just over a mile from the nearest college campus.

“Five years ago, Chinese chains barely registered in America. Now there are 86 of them running more than a thousand storefronts, and most Americans have walked past one without knowing where it came from,” said Daniel Silver, Retail Analyst at Chains From China. “This stopped being a trend story a while ago. It’s an industry, and nobody had actually counted it until now.”

“There are really two stories here, and they are not only about size. Some of these brands are built for everyone: the giants Miniso and Pop Mart, the tea and coffee chains, and even apparel brands that are big in China but still small in the US, like Balabala, JNBY, and EP Yaying, all opening in ordinary malls where most shoppers have no idea they are Chinese. Others, especially some restaurants, still cluster where Chinese and Asian communities already live, and the real question is which of them cross over,” Silver added.

## How the count is built

Chains From China compiles its census from two streams. Each day an automated collector reads the chains' own published store locators; separately, individual locations are confirmed by hand, through on-the-ground visits, operator-verified listings, and primary-source filings such as franchise disclosure documents. The two streams are complementary: automated collection provides breadth, while human verification provides the accuracy, resolving the namesakes, phantom listings, and scope questions that an automated pass cannot settle on its own. Notable observations and status changes, such as new openings, closures, and count revisions, are recorded in a tamper-evident, hash-chained provenance ledger, and most coordinates come from the US Census geocoder.

The count is deliberately conservative. Announced-but-unopened stores, automated vending kiosks, and locations verified as closed are all tracked, but kept out of the headline figure. The 1,152 count is a floor, not a ceiling. It includes only verified locations, and it keeps climbing as new stores open. The tracker is in beta, and its counts and map are updated continuously.

## A free public map, and more to come

The full census is browsable as a free interactive map at [chainsfromchina.com](https://chainsfromchina.com), which plots every outlet, lets readers filter by brand and sector, and surfaces the clusters where China-origin chains gather. A Pro tier adds revenue and capacity estimates, store-count comparisons against the brands' home markets, and co-tenancy analysis for operators, investors, and landlords.

The tracker updates daily, and Chains From China will issue periodic “state of the sector” briefings as the count moves. Reporters are welcome to cite the live figures; the public map and the headline counts are, and will remain, free.
